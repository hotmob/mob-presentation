# Content and narrative

Use this for new content, substantive review, or a changed conclusion. A typo fix does not need a new narrative exercise.

Start with the audience's question and the evidence available. Decide what each page contributes: a subject, explanation, comparison, finding or next action. Choose page order by dependencies in understanding, not a fixed sales story. Expert audiences may start with a mechanism or measurement; unfamiliar audiences may need a concrete situation first.

Audience roles change the questions, not the facts. Investors may need adoption, economics and uncertainty; partners may need complementary roles and commitment boundaries; product users may need a task, behavior and adoption path. These are prompts to inspect the brief, not mandatory section lists.

Preserve qualifications, attribution, units, date ranges and uncertainty. Distinguish a concept, prototype, test, pilot, deployment and measured outcome. Public market research cannot establish a private organization's results. If evidence supports only a narrower claim, narrow the claim or surface the missing evidence outside the draft.

For an existing deck, examine the real page order, notes, charts and image text. When restructuring, account for every source page: keep, revise, combine, move to appendix or omit, with a reason. Preserve necessary evidence and explanations through their new destination. Fewer slides alone is not a success criterion. Follow any content protection supplied by the caller.

Write a title that matches the page's job. A subject label can introduce a mechanism; a finding title needs evidence. Make the requested title/subtitle roles visible in their scale and placement. Do not turn a local title preference into a universal writing rule.

Diagrams should clarify an actual relationship: sequence, dependency, responsibility, comparison or feedback. Explain what an arrow means. Connected objects do not by themselves prove collaboration or value. A visual that suggests the wrong activity can overwhelm accurate accompanying text.

Use the level of detail required for the reading situation. Keep essential mechanism or business depth; explain it with hierarchy and examples instead of deleting it to make the page look simple. Speaking notes can add causes, transitions and caveats, but cannot repair a misleading visible claim. Write notes only when requested or needed for the actual use.

For language refinement, use an available writing skill when its specialist guidance helps. Keep its role bounded to expression; neither it nor this skill may supplement facts, change commitments or publish the result without authorization.
