# Content and narrative

Use this for new content, substantive review, or a changed conclusion. A typo fix does not need a new narrative exercise.

Start with the audience's question and the evidence available. Decide what each page contributes: a subject, explanation, comparison, finding or next action. Choose page order by dependencies in understanding, not a fixed sales story. Expert audiences may start with a mechanism or measurement; unfamiliar audiences may need a concrete situation first.

Audience roles change the questions, not the facts. Investors may need adoption, economics and uncertainty; partners may need complementary roles and commitment boundaries; product users may need a task, behavior and adoption path. These are prompts to inspect the brief, not mandatory section lists.

## Audience, claims and evidence

Separate the presenter's objective from the audience's own task, benefit and constraints. Establish what the audience already understands, which judgment needs to change, what evidence could support it and what useful action follows. Unknown motivations remain hypotheses; a job title does not establish them. Familiarity with a technology and business or domain expertise are independent. Communication stage and preferred evidence can affect explanation without dictating a fixed opening or page order.

For unfamiliar terminology, explain the relevant function before naming it; experts may need the precise term immediately. Do not explain one unfamiliar term with another. Examples and analogies must identify the relationship they illustrate and where the analogy stops working. They help comprehension but do not establish new facts. Keep the main argument intelligible without an appendix while retaining essential depth and qualifications.

Preserve qualifications, attribution, units, date ranges and uncertainty. Distinguish a concept, prototype, test, pilot, deployment and measured outcome. Public market research cannot establish a private organization's results. If evidence supports only a narrower claim, narrow the claim or surface the missing evidence outside the draft.

For a material quantitative claim, record its source/date, object or version, sample and conditions, metric/unit, result, supported conclusion and disclosure limits in the existing evidence record. Compare results only on compatible baselines and conditions; distinguish a supplier's result from an integration result or the presenter's own work. A single case proves only the tested scope, not general applicability or maturity. Photographs, measurements and relationship diagrams answer different questions and cannot substitute for each other's evidence.

For an existing deck, examine the real page order, notes, charts and image text. When restructuring, account for every source page: keep, revise, combine, move to appendix or omit, with a reason. Preserve necessary evidence and explanations through their new destination. Fewer slides alone is not a success criterion. Follow any content protection supplied by the caller.

A merge must identify the destination and how it still supports the original audience judgment. Record the loss when omitting material and the caller's required exception for protected content; do not lower its protection to fit a layout. Content categories and protection come from the actual brief, not a universal list. A coverage script checks completeness of records, not equivalence of the argument.

Write a title that matches the page's job. A subject label can introduce a mechanism; a finding title needs evidence. Make the requested title/subtitle roles visible in their scale and placement. Do not turn a local title preference into a universal writing rule.

Diagrams should clarify an actual relationship: sequence, dependency, responsibility, comparison or feedback. Explain what an arrow means. Connected objects do not by themselves prove collaboration or value. A visual that suggests the wrong activity can overwhelm accurate accompanying text.

Distinguish what a system does, how it does it, why it may improve and how it could create value. For a loop, identify inputs, actions and outputs; a feedback arrow alone does not prove compounding benefits. Label planned causal gains as hypotheses. The page's claim, supporting material and transition should each serve an identifiable audience judgment, without forcing every page into the same format.

Use the level of detail required for the reading situation. Keep essential mechanism or business depth; explain it with hierarchy and examples instead of deleting it to make the page look simple. Speaking notes can add causes, transitions and caveats, but cannot repair a misleading visible claim. Write notes only when requested or needed for the actual use.

For language refinement, use an available writing skill when its specialist guidance helps. Keep its role bounded to expression; neither it nor this skill may supplement facts, change commitments or publish the result without authorization.

## Speaking notes and reading copies

Distinguish a speaking script or outline from source annotations. Agree on the needed use; notes do not automatically require a word-for-word script. Decide what the audience sees, what the speaker adds and why the next segment follows. Do not merely expand or repeat the slide. Use clear references, natural causes and transitions; pauses, demonstrations and recaps need not introduce a new claim. Do not copy a reference speaker's biography, verbal habits or errors.

Keep names, numbers, dates, maturity and commitment strength consistent between visible content and notes. A visible proposal must not become an achieved result in speech. Material limitations belong where the audience can understand them, not only in hidden notes. Keep internal review instructions separate from speaking and source material.

Review the continuity of the intended talk, or the affected segment and real dependencies for a local edit. Check a reading copy without relying on a speaker; a dual-use deck needs both checks. Word-count estimates can reveal timing risk but are not a timed rehearsal. Label text-only continuity review honestly; use actual reading, audio or timing evidence when rehearsal is requested or needed.

When anonymizing or correcting a recurring name, inspect relevant slide text, notes, image text, links and distributed copies. Preserve permitted internal source attribution separately; do not reintroduce a private identity into the outward artifact through a note or attachment.
