# Quality, verification and learning

## Work on a visible problem

Identify the reading failure and its consequence before selecting a visual treatment. Match layout to the relationship: comparison, sequence, mechanism, evidence or scene. Use real references to calibrate composition and density, with their provenance and applicable constraints.

When direction is uncertain, test the important unresolved choice on representative content. A difficult content page often reveals more than a cover. Compare a small number only when the alternatives help make a decision; there is no compulsory number of directions, images or full decks. Fixed templates and local edits retain the current design unless the request changes it.

Specify usable page dimensions, typography, spacing, color meaning and asset handling in the caller's design record. Choose actual sizes for the template and viewing environment; do not prescribe one universal point-size threshold. Confirm installed fonts and inspect replacement behavior where possible.

Check the exported text properties as well as the pixels: source CSS pixels are not automatically PowerPoint points. A title/subtitle role must match actual scale, weight and placement. If font substitution or multiline layout is uncertain, use a second supported Office rendering path on the affected pages when available, recording what remains untested. A denser reading copy is not automatically suitable for distant projection.

Vary composition when the information relationship requires it; do not force every mechanism, story and comparison into the same card grid. Record the specific reference principle adopted, where it appears and whether the rendered content supports the choice. Compare directions on stable content, and never record an agent's selection as a person's decision.

## Separate judgments

| Judgment | Evidence | A pass does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | Sources, wording, units and preserved qualifications | Visual clarity |
| Communication | Actual slide and adjacent flow; whether the claim and visual relationship agree | Target audience understanding unless tested with that audience |
| Visual quality | Full-size renders, reference/current-best comparison, hierarchy, composition, legibility and consistency | Correct package structure or source truth |
| Engineering | Package/open checks, relationships, object types, text/notes readback, required checks for the change | Good design or acceptance |
| Delivery | Exact artifact/version, requested format and destination, remaining limits | Approval beyond the decision actually given |

Model first-impression checks are simulated reviews. Record them as such. Different reviewers or numerical scores may reveal issues but do not replace inspection or audience evidence.

When actual audience feedback exists, retain the artifact version, audience/context, what they understood or misread, their questions and relevant next action. A sale or agreement has other causes and cannot be attributed to a deck alone. Specialist review should answer concrete questions with evidence, not perform a procession of fictional roles or substitute a majority vote for findings.

## Inspect, compare, revise

1. Render the exact candidate through the selected owner's supported path. View the actual pixels; generated filenames alone are not inspection evidence.
2. Check the message at normal reading scale, then details: clipping, overlap, unintended line breaks, missing assets, contrast, chart labels, font substitution and misleading imagery.
3. Compare with the requested reference and retained best version under equivalent conditions. Include neighboring slides if the change affects the sequence. Record why the candidate improves the stated goal, or why it does not.
4. If it fails, keep the best version. Revise the owning source or composition. A failed hypothesis is a next test, not a proven general rule. Do not promote a weaker candidate simply because it is newer or technically valid.
5. Rerun the affected rendering and checks after changes. Stop broadening tests once the relevant concern is resolved; honor mandatory checks supplied by the caller.

Full creation/rebuild requires all page renders and inspection. For a bounded change, inspect affected pages and dependencies, preserve unrelated content where required, and state the narrower coverage. PDF or browser rendering does not prove a deck was opened in Microsoft PowerPoint. File checks cannot establish font behavior on another machine.

An unavailable renderer is a real completion gap for visual acceptance. Continue source inspection or other independent work, but do not report a visually verified final deck. A draft can be provided when the caller permits it and the limitation is visible.

## Handoff and learning

Include only requested files and limitations affecting use. Source identity, renderer/version, changed pages, unresolved findings and relevant technical evidence go in the existing private work record when needed. Keep process language out of audience-facing slides and notes unless requested.

Compare requested copies with the final editable source for page count, proportions and content. Reconcile stale specifications and plans against the actual artifact before relying on them, preserving the history of decisions rather than manufacturing earlier acceptance. Keep valid upstream decisions; a changed brief, claim or structure requires rechecking dependent outputs. Authorized iteration can proceed on an explicitly labelled candidate baseline without inventing a human approval.

Capture a reusable lesson with: the observed failure, before/after evidence, the condition under which the change helped, counterexamples or limits, and whether it was technical verification, simulated review, human feedback or actual use. Keep source facts and brand choices at their existing owner. Propose a general rule only when evidence supports its scope; label untested explanations as hypotheses.

No fixed preference for a tool, template, color, page count or metaphor follows from one successful page. Do not copy private review history into a public skill or issue. Use a synthetic illustration or cleared evidence when contributing improvements.

After a method change, use a few realistic requests to check route selection, inputs consulted, missing information and intended output. Include a bounded edit, a structural rewrite and a factual-boundary case. Prefer an independent reading when available; identify a self-walkthrough as such and do not call it blinded evaluation. Correct observed failures and repeat only affected cases.
